Apps

The whole app list, on the watch

Every one of these runs on the watch itself, with no phone needed and no download. Swipe up from the watch face to get the list. MintOS reorders it around what you actually open.

Watch face

The Mint One face: hours in white over mint minutes, date, and a glance row underneath. Activity rings wrap the outside. Tap through to any of the faces MintOS has picked for you.

Activities

112 activity types. Eight get a tuned screen and metric set, the other 104 are in the list. Pick one, hit start, and the dial turns into the numbers that matter for that sport.

112 activities

Heart Rate

A live trace of your actual beat, not a number that updates every minute. Resting, current and the day's range, with the whole P wave through T wave shape drawn as it happens.

Health

One scrollable dashboard: readiness ring, last night's sleep, a heart rate sparkline, skin temperature, steps and effort. Everything in one scroll instead of five separate apps.

Breathe

A guided circle on 4 seconds in, 4 hold, 6 out: a 14 second cycle, a little over 4 breaths a minute. Tap to begin and the haptics keep time so you can close your eyes.

Sleep

Deep, REM, light and awake laid out as a stage bar for the night, with a sleep score and how long you spent in each. It works out when you fell asleep on its own.

Compass

A proper rotating tick ring with a fixed needle, plus your heading in degrees. The one you want when the trail sign has been knocked over.

World Clock

5 zones at a glance: local plus New York, London, Dubai and Tokyo, with a marker when a city has already rolled into tomorrow or is still on yesterday.

Timer and stopwatch

Set it by voice or by dial, and it buzzes when it is done. Stopwatch start, stop and reset answer instantly without waking anything up.

Metronome

A swinging pendulum with tap tempo, plus 6 named presets from Largo to Presto. Haptic clicks so you can practise without the noise.

Level

A bubble level on your wrist, reading level, up, down, left, right and the diagonals. Surprisingly hard to live without once you have hung one shelf with it.

Dice

Roll one, keep a running count. It is there because sometimes you need to settle something and nobody has a die.

MintAI

The assistant, with its own screen. Speak or type, and it answers on the dial and does the thing you asked for. It shows you whether it answered on the watch or reached for the cloud.

MintAI

An assistant that lives on the watch

MintAI answers on three levels, and it always starts with the cheapest one that can do the job. You never pick. It just picks well.

  • Instant answers, no thinking required. Time, date, heart rate, steps, battery, brightness, your activity so far, timers and the stopwatch come straight back. Nothing wakes up, nothing spins, nothing costs you anything.
  • The model on the watch. A roughly 2 billion parameter model on the AI core inside the case. It boots in 1.6 seconds, stays warm for 25 seconds after you stop talking, then powers all the way off. Free, unlimited, and it works in a tunnel.
  • The bigger model in the cloud. Roughly 9 billion parameters, for the hard questions, and only when you allow it. It is metered, so MintAI will not reach for it quietly. It also costs less battery than thinking locally, because only the radio has to wake rather than the whole AI core.
MintAI tells you which of the three answered. There is no silent upgrade to the paid tier.

Health

What it measures, around the clock

The optical array in the caseback runs day and night. You can turn any of it off, and you can set how often it samples.

Heart rate

3 sampling modes: continuous, every 10 minutes, or on demand. The choice moves battery life by days, not hours.

Resting heart rate

Tracked as a trend, because the direction it moves says more than any single morning reading.

Heart rate variability

The number underneath readiness. It is how the watch notices you are run down before you do.

Blood oxygen

SpO2 from the same array, spot checked or overnight. Reads low and you get told, not buried in a chart.

Skin temperature

Deviation from your own baseline, which is the part that matters. A rise before a cold is a real signal.

Readiness

One number for how much you have in the tank today, built from sleep, HRV, resting heart rate and yesterday's load.

Effort and fatigue

How hard you have gone recently and how much of it you have paid back. It is what stops the coach pushing you into a hole.

Steps and distance

The basics, done properly, with distance from GPS when you are outside and from your stride when you are not.

Stress

Estimated from heart rate variability through the day, so you can see the meeting that did it.

MintAI is not a doctor and will not pretend to be one. It will tell you plainly when a reading is worth getting checked in person, and it will not name a condition or a dose from a wrist sensor.
Fitness

Coaching that works backward from your goal

Tell it what you want. It figures out today from there, and it rewrites the plan when your week does not go to plan.

  • A real goal, not a step target. A lift number, a race distance, a weight, a date. It works out what today has to look like.
  • Daily adjustment. Slept badly, missed a session, went too hard on Sunday: the plan moves rather than judging you.
  • Live cues. Short prompts mid session about pace and heart rate zone, on the dial and in your wrist.
  • Form on the dial. A 3D render of the movement, playing on your wrist, when you are not sure how the lift should look.
  • Food logging by voice. Say what you ate and it lands with calories and macros. No searching a database.

One number for the night, with the time in each stage underneath it rather than hidden a tap away.

Overnight vitals

Heart rate, HRV, blood oxygen and skin temperature through the night, which is when they are most honest.

It feeds tomorrow

Modes

Six modes, and they actually change things

One is active at a time. Each one really does move brightness, sensor duty, the AI core and the notification policy, rather than just setting a flag.

Normal

Everything on, MintAI available on the watch. About 6 days with the always-on display, about 9 with it off.

~6 days

Battery saver

Display down to about 35 percent, sensors duty cycled, AI core forced off. Instant answers still work.

Up to 15 days

Expedition

Display at about 20 percent, everything non essential asleep. The setting for a trip with no charger in it.

30+ days

Theater

Held at an 8 percent floor and silent, then it peeks to 55 percent for 4 seconds on any press and goes dark again. Nobody behind you sees a thing.

Water lock

Touch off so water cannot tap things, buttons still work. Hold to unlock when you are out.

Do not disturb

Notifications go silent, timers hold their alerts, and the watch face shows a badge so you remember it is on.

Safety

Fall detection

A hard fall reads differently from setting a bag down. It knows the difference and gives you the chance to say you are fine.

Crash detection

The accelerometer sees the deceleration of a car crash. If you cannot answer, it acts.

Heart rhythm watch

A rhythm that is out of character for you gets flagged, not a generic threshold applied to everybody.

It makes the call

MintAI calls emergency services and speaks to the dispatcher for you, with where you are and what happened.

Medical details ready

Your conditions, medications and emergency contacts handed over so responders arrive knowing.

It reaches further

Deep in a canyon, underground, far from a tower. It gets a message out where a phone gives up.

Crisis support done right

MintAI knows the difference between a medical emergency and a mental health one, and points at the right help rather than the nearest number.

Cannot be talked around

The safety behaviour is fixed, not a personality setting. It holds even when you ask it not to.

Getting around

The whole system is reachable from the watch face without learning anything. Everything goes back the same way.

  • Swipe up for the app list.
  • Swipe down for the quick panel.
  • Swipe left for MintAI.
  • Swipe right to go back, from anywhere, every time.

Privacy

Your data stays on your wrist

This is an architecture decision, not a policy promise. The assistant runs on the watch, so there is nothing that has to leave it.

On-device by default

Your health, your voice and your habits are processed on the watch. The default setting is on-device only.

Cloud is opt in

It exists, it is metered, and it is off unless you turn it on. MintAI will not reach for it quietly.

A hardware mic mute

Cuts the microphone at the hardware switch. Voice stops working, which is exactly how you know it worked.

Post-quantum crypto

ML-KEM for key exchange and ML-DSA for signatures, the two NIST standards chosen to survive quantum computers. No classical-only fallback.

Nothing sold

No data brokers, no ad profiles, no "anonymised partners". There is no business model here that needs your sleep data.

You can see it all

Every number the watch holds is readable on the watch, and exportable when you want it elsewhere.

Battery

Days, and honest numbers

Two chips instead of one is the reason. A tiny one runs the watch all day at a couple of milliwatts. The big AI chip is completely powered off between questions rather than idling.

  • About 6 days in normal use with the always-on display running.
  • About 9 days with the always-on display off.
  • Up to 15 days in power saver, where the AI core is off and instant answers still work.
  • 30 days and more in expedition mode, for trips without a charger.
  • Wireless charging on a puck you drop it onto at any angle. Nothing to plug in, nothing to wear out.
The watch itself runs all day on about 2 mW. The AI chip boots in 1.6 seconds, holds warm for 25 seconds after you stop talking, then powers down completely rather than idling. Standby is roughly two thirds of the daily energy, so that one decision is where the days come from.
Hardware

What it is made of

Machined, not moulded. Sealed, not gasketed and hoped for.

Case
47.0 mm outer diameter, 45.0 mm internal cavity, 15.0 mm assembled height. Machined titanium with a ceramic MAO finish, grown out of the metal rather than coated onto it
Display
1.39 inch AMOLED, 454 × 454, 35.4 mm active area, always-on capable
Crystal
42.2 mm sapphire, 1.15 mm thick, doming 0.55 mm above the bezel. Bezel aperture 40.8 mm
Buttons
3 sealed inductive buttons at 60, 90 and 120 degrees from twelve. Pads are 7.0 × 4.0 mm, and 7.0 × 4.6 mm for the mint primary, standing 0.42 mm proud on a 0.04 mm skirt gap. Zero holes through the case wall
Water resistance
15 ATM. The pool, the sea, the shower, and diving
Strap
24 mm fluoroelastomer on 1.3 mm quick-release spring bars, so a swap takes seconds and no tool
Sensors
25 mm optical array for heart rate and blood oxygen, plus skin temperature, a 6-axis accelerometer and gyroscope, ambient light, barometer and magnetometer
Caseback
1.7 mm plate over a 25.2 mm sensor puck standing 2.0 mm proud, so the optics sit against skin rather than looking across a gap
Fasteners
8 stainless M1.6 × 3 mm Torx. Four into the caseback on a 19.6 mm bolt circle, four through the lugs, all seated flush
Audio
Speaker and microphone on board, with a hardware mute for the mic
Compute
Two chips. A low-power controller runs the entire watch at about 2 mW, and a separate applications-class AI core powers up only to think, then powers off rather than idling
Battery
1000 mAh silicon-carbon cell, about 3.8 Wh, charged wirelessly
Colours
4 at launch: Midnight, Chalk, Forest and Copper. Final list confirmed closer to production
In the box
MintWatch, strap fitted, and the wireless charging puck
MintWatch is still in development, so some of these numbers will move before it ships. When one does, we will say so here rather than quietly editing the page.
